## Purpose

KERN is an open, modular UX standard for accessible and user-centred digital services across German federal, state, and municipal administration. It combines a design system, methods, community practice, and platform-independent implementation.

Official overview: https://www.kern-ux.de/ueber-kern/

## Four design principles

1. **Develop further instead of rebuilding**: Begin with proven components and patterns. Adapt or create only for a demonstrated contextual need, and make reusable results available.
2. **Needs-oriented and inclusive**: Base decisions on qualitative and quantitative evidence. Account for diverse capabilities, contexts, language skills, devices, and frequency of use.
3. **Be courageous and learn**: Start small, test early, reduce to essentials, and iterate from evidence. Discard concepts that do not serve users.
4. **Transparent**: Explain current state, open issues, consequences, and next steps. Let users control how much detail they open. Transparency builds trust.

Official principles: https://www.kern-ux.de/develop/design-system/gestaltungsprinzipien/

## User-centred lifecycle

Use five connected phases:

1. **Initialisation**: Define purpose, measurable goals, scope, UX strategy, stakeholders, target groups, constraints, plan, and methods.
2. **Exploration**: Understand users, staff, providers, ecosystem, tasks, processes, artefacts, law, and technical constraints. Use research rather than assumptions.
3. **Design**: Turn needs into journeys, information architecture, sketches, wireframes, mock-ups, and prototypes. Test with users and stakeholders.
4. **Development**: Implement collaboratively, preserve design intent, and run usability and accessibility checks throughout development.
5. **Operation**: Monitor real use, support needs, errors, and changing requirements; continue research and iteration.

Include people with disabilities and extreme usage contexts in research. Consider first-time and infrequent users, limited domain knowledge, language variation, assistive technology, mobile use, stress, and interrupted workflows.

Official method: https://www.kern-ux.de/methoden-und-beratung/nutzerinnenzentrierte-loesungen-erstellen/

## Language and service behaviour

- State the purpose and expected outcome before requesting data.
- Use familiar terms and explain administrative or legal terms where first needed.
- Ask only information required at the current stage.
- Break long applications into coherent sections and preserve entered data.
- Explain why sensitive or unexpected information is needed.
- Make errors specific, non-blaming, and recoverable.
- Confirm consequential actions and provide a summary before final submission.
- Communicate processing status, responsibility, deadlines, and next steps.
- Do not hide essential information behind optional disclosure.
